Game Design

Everything in Sky Kitchen, how it works and why.

Contents Overview Design pillars Core loop Ingredients Machines & recipes Kitchen tools The sky Power-ups & boosters Levels Economy The chef Under the hood

1Overview

Ingredients rain continuously from the sky. The player places kitchen tools in the air to steer them into machines, and the production line they build has to fill the order before the clock runs out.

GenrePhysics puzzle, hybrid casual: a production-line game in the sky
PlatformAndroid, portrait, one hand
SessionLevels of about one minute, with a short setup countdown before the clock starts
Content50 levels in the Sky world; the Space world (levels 51–100) is coming
Business modelFree. Rewarded ads only, always the player's choice. No purchases
Audience13+, casual puzzle players who like to tinker
EngineUnity 6, URP, PhysX
What it is not: a build-then-watch puzzle. Food never stops falling, so Sky Kitchen is a throughput game. A missed fruit never ends the level; it only slows the order. The player never hits a wall, and a better line is always one tweak away.

2Design pillars

01

Trying is free

A tool can be moved, turned or taken back at any moment, and it returns to stock in full, instantly. A level hands out a stock of tools; it is spent on placing, never on trying.

02

Honest physics

Food moves only by physics: real bounces, slides and rolls. No tool carries food along a scripted path, so what the player sees is always what really happened.

03

One angle, one outcome

Tools turn in nine fixed steps, from flat to steep, left and right. A chef's hat always throws along the way it faces, wherever the food came from. That is where learnability comes from.

04

Forgiving targets

Machine mouths are wide funnels, nearly six fruits across: hitting the neighbourhood is enough. The delivery bin is wide too. The puzzle is routing, not aiming.

3Core loop

Read the order

The chef's briefing shows the drinks to make, the time, the tools in stock and any bonus.

Build

Drag tools into the sky and turn them, during the setup countdown and while the clock runs.

Watch & tweak

Food flows through the line. Pause, play at 1x or 2x, and fix what misses.

Deliver

Products fall into the bin. Fill every line of the order before time runs out to win coins.

The clock is the player's

Stop, 1x or 2x. Stopping time freezes the food in mid-air, so the player can study the line and plan the next tool without pressure. Building never needs the clock stopped.

The line tells you what is wrong

Each machine wears a status ring: green when working, amber when starving, red when blocked. A problem off-screen shows an arrow at the edge; a tap slides the camera to it.

Wrong ingredients bounce back

Feed a machine something it cannot use and it throws it straight back at the player, and the machine pays a short delay before its next product.

The bonus

An order line can carry a bonus: deliver a set number of that drink and an extra tool arrives, once. It is the only way stock grows during a level, and it is always stated before the level starts.

4Ingredients

Ingredients fall from droppers at the top of the level, one at a time, on a steady beat. They are free and never run out; what is scarce is how fast they come. Every new food is introduced by the chef the first time it appears.

The first fruit. Makes banana milkshakes, and later banana ice cream.

The small fruit. It falls twice as often as the others, and a shake takes two, so a strawberry line has twice as much in the air.

5Machines & recipes

One machine per kind of product, and the fruit decides the flavour. A milkshake machine makes every milkshake: feed it a banana and it makes a banana shake, feed it a mango and it makes a mango shake. The player never opens a recipe menu; they control what comes out by controlling what goes in. Machines are placed by the level. From level 6 the player can move them, but never remove them.

Level 13 s cycle

Milkshake machine

Fruit + milk. Takes banana, strawberry and mango.

Level 94 s cycle

Juicer

Fruit + water. Takes apple and mango. The slower machine, so it reads as grinding.

Level 263 s cycle

Ice cream machine

Fruit + cream. Takes banana, strawberry and mango. The end of the game's first chain.

Level 262 s cycle

Cream machine

Milk in, cream out. The small helper: a short cycle so it keeps up with the line it feeds.

The oldest finished recipe goes first. A machine makes the first recipe it has every ingredient for, in the order they arrived. A lone strawberry waiting for its partner never holds up the banana behind it.
Delivery. Products fall into a wide bin under the line. A sorter under the bin sends what the order still needs along its pipe, where it counts, and throws everything else out of its spout, so the player sees every miss happen.

6Kitchen tools

The player's tools are kitchen objects, each chosen so its shape says what it does. A level hands out a stock of them; a tool can be placed, moved, turned and taken back at any time.

Level 1Steers

Pizza peel

The ramp. Food lands on it and slides off along its slope. Nine angles, from flat to steep, both ways. Laid flat, it holds what falls on it.

Level 5Throws

Chef's hat

The trampoline. It always launches food along the way it faces, whatever angle the food came from. The tool that throws things up and over.

Level 16Turns

Macaroni

A pasta elbow, a real tube. Food falls into one end, rolls round the bend and comes out of the other: a 90° turn. Faster after going down the bend, slower after going up. The only tool that turns a full circle.

Level 23Lifts

Kettle

The first tool that lifts. Its spout blows a jet of rising steam: food crossing it gets a nudge, food riding along it is thrown. The longer food stays in the steam, the harder it is pushed.

Level 28Speeds up

Butter

Slippery. Anything that slides over it gets the same boost of speed, once per touch, along the way it is already going. Sends food across the sky.

Level 33Slows

Sponge

The butter's opposite. It soaks up a landing, so food never bounces off it, and on it speed is gained slowly and lost fast. The tool for a short, soft throw.

7The sky

The level's own terrain. There is no decoration in Sky Kitchen: everything in the sky does something to the food. The player cannot pick terrain up, so it is the puzzle the tools are built around. Levels come by day and by night, where the moon, the stars and the machines' lamps are the only light.

Level 2Fixed

Asteroids

Flat ones are ledges: food lands and slides off. Round ones send it glancing away. Slippery on purpose, so nothing piles up on them.

Level 13Turns

Turning asteroid

A tilted slab that turns like a record. Half of each turn it sheds food one way and half the other, so one stream comes off left, right, left, right.

Fixed

Curved asteroid

A long, bent ridge of rock that bends a stream along its curve.

Brake

Cloud

Soft but solid: it takes nine tenths of the speed out of whatever hits it. A cloud over a machine slows its deliveries too.

Level 7Floats

Satellite parts

Eight parts of a wrecked satellite, floating free. Every hit pushes and turns them, so a layout built around one slowly drifts, and moving air carries them away.

Level 14Steals

Eagle's nest

A hole in the line you can see. Food that lands in the bowl is snatched a second later by an eagle swooping in from off-screen. The hammer can smash it.

NightFixed

Moon

Lights a night level, and is an obstacle too. Food passes through its bite; turned horns-up it becomes a cup.

NightSpins

Star

Pinned like a wheel on its axle. A hit spins it, and while it turns its points bat the next fruit off in a direction that depends on when it arrives.

Level 18Gathers

Tornado

Catches whatever falls into its funnel, spins it round and drops it straight down out of its tip, one at a time, from the same point every time. It turns a wide stream into one.

Level 21Air

Wind

Air, not an obstacle. Everything inside the band is carried along its arrow at the same speed, whatever its weight. Tools can be placed inside it.

Level 22Air

Updraft

A wind that blows up. Food rises and hovers at its top like a ball on a hair dryer, which is exactly where a peel goes.

Level 27Hovers

Spacecraft

A little craft hovering in place with a gentle bob, a solid obstacle with a hum of its own.

Level 31Swings

Kite

Flies a figure-eight on its string on a fixed beat, and food bounces off it: a ramp that keeps changing its angle and height. Because the beat is fixed, a build can be timed to it.

Level 36Carries

Rainbow

A one-way slide. Wherever food lands on the band, it slides over and leaves at the far foot, always at the same place, slowed by the cloud there.

Level 38Strikes

Storm cloud

Rain falls from it, and lightning strikes some of the food that comes into the rain, freezing it for a moment and knocking it straight down. Only a strike makes a sound.

Level 41Hides

Fog

Hides part of the level until it clears. It blocks nothing, and tools can be built inside it, but what it hides has to be worked out from what comes out.

8Power-ups & boosters

Power-ups are used during a level; boosters are picked before it starts. Each one arrives with three free uses on the level that introduces it, and the chef explains it there. More can be bought with coins.

FreeLevel 1

Analyze

Slows the whole game, music included, to half speed for 5 seconds, so the player can follow food that falls too fast to see. Then it rests for 10 seconds. Always free.

Power-upLevel 6

Move

Opens a 15-second window in which every machine can be dragged to a new place. Machines move freely during setup; after that, this is the way.

Power-upLevel 8

Hammer

Smashes an obstacle out of the sky: an asteroid in the way, or an eagle's nest stealing the food.

Power-upLevel 10

+10 seconds

Adds ten seconds to the clock, for the order that is almost done.

BoosterLevel 11

Extra tool

Starts the level with one more tool in stock.

BoosterLevel 13

+20 seconds

Starts the level with twenty more seconds on the clock.

BoosterLevel 15

Faster machine

The level's machines work faster, so the line keeps up with a heavy stream.

9Levels

50 hand-built levels in the Sky world. The first ten alternate tutorial and free levels, and the difficulty starts after level 15. After that the game moves in blocks of five: one new thing, then practice. A tutorial teaches the control, never the answer: the chef never shows where a tool goes or at what angle.

Kitchen tool Sky obstacle Food or machine Power-up or booster Practice

Levels 51–100: the Space world, coming soon.

Variety without new rules

Night levels, and two-floor levels where the line runs a whole screen down, give old pieces new problems. A level is one screen wide and the camera scrolls up and down only, so the line can never be lost sideways.

The journey and the recipe book

The journey shows one card per world. The recipe book collects every drink: one painted page per world, and a recipe not made yet is a dark silhouette until the kitchen first makes it.

10Economy

One currency, coins, priced off one base: 150 coins, the worth of one ad. Nothing is sold for real money. Ads are rewarded only, always the player's choice, in three places.

EarnCoins
A level won (once per level)+100
Daily coins, seven days in a row (a missed day starts again)+50 → 350
Watch an ad in the market+150
SpendCoins
Any power-up or booster150
One heart150 or an ad
Continue: +20 seconds when the clock runs out300 or an ad

Hearts

Five hearts, one back every 10 minutes. A heart is spent only when a lost level is given up, never just because the clock ran out, so the continue offer comes first.

Owned, not rented

Power-ups and boosters are counts the player owns and keeps between levels, and as many can be used in a level as are owned.

11The chef

The chef is the game's voice. Every line the game says comes out of his speech bubble: he introduces each new food the first time it falls, teaches each control once, and names every power-up on its first level.

During a level he lives in a badge on the top bar, reacting as it happens: delighted at a delivery, worried as the clock runs low, cheering a bonus and celebrating the win with a "Woo-hoo!". On the pages around a level he appears in person, talking with his mouth moving to his babble.

12Under the hood

3D objects, 2D play

Everything is 3D and lit, but motion happens on one flat plane, run by 3D PhysX. Tested on a mid-range Android phone: 300 objects in motion at a steady 60 fps.

One material

Every model is coloured from one small palette texture and shares one material, so the whole kitchen draws in very few batches.

Every sound in one key

Every in-level sound is tuned to the level music's key and levelled for a phone speaker. Each new object gets a sound of its own, of what it is made of: the eagle's cry, the kettle's hiss, the macaroni's slurp.

Built to be tuned

Anonymous level statistics show where players stop and why they fail, so levels that are too hard get easier. The game plays fully offline.
